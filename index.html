Why this program

This program places teachers’ wellbeing, self-understanding and personal growth at the center of leadership development. Teachers bring their histories, aspirations, habits and emotional responses into their relationships with others. Their wellbeing matters in its own right and shapes how they experience their responsibilities within families, institutions and communities. The program provides space to examine how they think, respond to difficulties and exercise authority.

The theory

The prosocial approach

The connection between teachers’ wellbeing and students’ educational experience has a theoretical basis. Jennings and Greenberg’s ‘Prosocial Classroom’ model proposes that teachers’ wellbeing and social and emotional competence contribute to supportive teacher–student relationships and the classroom environment. Applied to higher education, this perspective focuses on how teachers respond to mistakes, give feedback, handle disagreement, and exercise judgment under pressure. Supporting teachers’ wellbeing, alongside developing these capacities, can help create conditions in which students feel able to ask questions, seek assistance, and engage in learning.

Personal wellbeing

Teachers also need an understanding of what wellbeing involves and how relationships and institutions shape it. Self-determination theory identifies autonomy, competence, and relatedness - the experience of choice, capability, and connection - as psychological needs that support motivation and wellbeing. This framework helps connect teachers’ own experiences with those of students. Reflecting on when they feel trusted, capable, heard, or excluded can help teachers recognize how their teaching, supervision, and administrative decisions affect others. It also encourages institutions to examine whether their practices support these needs among both faculty and students.

The leader’s own wellbeing

Capacities that benefit those who practice them

These leadership capacities also benefit those who practice them. Empathy can help participants understand situations before reacting; collegiality can build relationships through which they receive support, exchange ideas, and share responsibilities. Acting with integrity can strengthen self-respect and trust in their judgment. These capacities help participants navigate disagreement, acknowledge mistakes, and make decisions they can stand by. Caring for these relationships is also an investment in their own wellbeing, sense of belonging and ability to sustain a meaningful life.

Connections in practice

Participants will explore these connections through self-awareness, communication and teamwork, critical thinking, and ethical leadership. Conversation, art, sport, theatre and reflection will provide opportunities to practice listening, expressing needs, setting boundaries, acknowledging mistakes and repairing relationships. Personal change will be connected to collective responsibility: workload, institutional expectations, fairness and access to support must also receive attention. Each participant will identify changes in themselves and in practices they can influence. The purpose is to help teachers sustain their wellbeing and build relationships across their lives, while contributing to an institutional culture in which teachers and students can learn, participate and flourish.
